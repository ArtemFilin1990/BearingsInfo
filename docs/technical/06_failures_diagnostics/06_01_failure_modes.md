# Bearing Failure Modes

## Purpose
This document provides comprehensive coverage of bearing failure modes, their causes, visual identification, and prevention strategies. Based on industry standards (ISO 15243, ГОСТ 20407-75) and manufacturer experience (SKF, FAG, NSK, Timken).

## Overview of Bearing Failures

### Failure Statistics
According to SKF and industry studies, typical bearing failure distribution:
- **Inadequate lubrication:** 36%
- **Contamination:** 14%
- **Improper mounting:** 16%
- **Fatigue (normal wear-out):** 34%

**Note:** Only ~34% of failures are due to normal fatigue at or beyond calculated L10 life. The majority (66%) are premature failures due to avoidable conditions.

### Failure Analysis Approach
1. **Visual examination** - Document damage patterns, location, severity
2. **Operational history review** - Load, speed, temperature, lubrication
3. **Dimensional inspection** - Measure wear, deformation, clearance
4. **Metallurgical analysis** - Material defects, heat treatment (if needed)
5. **Root cause determination** - Identify primary and contributing factors

## 1. Fatigue Failures

### 1.1 Normal Fatigue (Усталостное выкрашивание)

**Description:**
Rolling contact fatigue is the progressive structural deterioration of bearing material due to cyclic stress. This is the expected failure mode when bearing reaches its calculated life.

**Mechanism:**
- Subsurface stress cycling creates micro-cracks
- Cracks propagate to surface under continued loading
- Material particles detach, forming characteristic spalls
- Process accelerates once initiated

**Visual Appearance:**
- Shallow spalling on raceways or rolling elements
- Initiated subsurface, propagates to surface
- Shiny facets with sharp edges at spall boundaries
- Typically starts in maximum stress zone (load zone)
- Progresses circumferentially and axially

**Location Patterns:**
- **Stationary outer ring:** Spalling in narrow band (load zone)
- **Rotating inner ring:** Spalling distributed around full circumference
- **Ball bearings:** Typically starts on one ring first
- **Roller bearings:** May initiate on rollers or raceways

**Typical Timeline:**
- Initiation: 80-95% of bearing life
- Visible spalling: 95-100% of life
- Rapid deterioration: After initial spalling detected

**Acceptance:**
- Normal fatigue at or beyond L10 life is acceptable
- Indicates proper bearing selection and operation
- Plan replacement during next scheduled maintenance

**Prevention:**
- Proper bearing selection for load and life requirements
- Adequate lubrication film thickness (λ ratio > 1)
- Controlled operating conditions
- Preventive replacement based on condition monitoring

**ISO 15243 Classification:** Fatigue - typical appearance

### 1.2 Subsurface-Initiated Fatigue

**Description:**
Fatigue originating below the surface due to maximum subsurface shear stress or material inclusions.

**Causes:**
- High contact stresses exceeding material endurance
- Non-metallic inclusions creating stress concentrations
- Material inhomogeneity
- Hydrogen embrittlement
- Inadequate heat treatment

**Visual Appearance:**
- Large, deep spalls
- Often irregular shape
- May expose inclusion or void at spall origin
- Darker appearance at bottom of spall
- Can extend deeply into raceway

**Distinction from Surface-Initiated:**
- Deeper spalls
- Often starts at inclusion site
- May show butterfly-pattern cracking in metallurgical section
- Typically faster progression

**Prevention:**
- High-quality bearing materials with low inclusion count
- Proper heat treatment and microstructure
- Adequate material hardness (58-65 HRC for raceways)
- Avoid overloading beyond C0 static rating

**ГОСТ 20407-75 Classification:** Усталостное выкрашивание

### 1.3 Pitting (Питтинг)

**Description:**
Small, shallow craters on raceway surfaces, often a precursor to spalling.

**Types:**

#### Initial Pitting
- Very small depressions (< 1mm)
- May occur during run-in period
- Can stabilize if conditions are good
- Not necessarily progressive

#### Progressive Pitting
- Continued pit formation and growth
- Indicates inadequate lubrication or contamination
- Will develop into spalling
- Requires immediate attention

**Visual Appearance:**
- Multiple small craters
- Dull, frosted appearance on otherwise polished raceway
- May be accompanied by discoloration
- Random or clustered distribution

**Causes:**
- Thin lubrication film (insufficient λ ratio)
- Surface asperity contact
- Contamination particles
- Moisture in lubricant
- Excessive slip in rolling contact

**Prevention:**
- Ensure adequate lubrication film thickness
- Use proper viscosity lubricant
- Maintain cleanliness
- Control operating temperature
- Proper preload adjustment

### 1.4 Micropitting

**Description:**
Very fine surface fatigue with pits typically < 10μm, creating a grey, matte surface finish.

**Characteristics:**
- Extremely fine texture
- Grey, frosted appearance
- Caused by very thin lubricant films
- Common in roller bearings under marginal lubrication

**Operating Conditions:**
- Low lubricant viscosity
- High speeds reducing film thickness
- High contact pressures
- Poor surface finish
- Contamination

**Detection:**
- Visual inspection shows dulling
- Roughness measurement shows increased Ra
- Vibration analysis may not detect early stages

**Action Required:**
- Improve lubrication (increase viscosity)
- Reduce operating temperature
- Improve filtration
- Consider EP additives
- May stabilize if conditions corrected

## 2. Wear Failures

### 2.1 Abrasive Wear (Абразивный износ)

**Description:**
Material removal by hard particles trapped in bearing or lubricant.

**Mechanism:**
- Hard particles (dirt, wear debris, metal particles) act as abrasive
- Particles score and groove softer bearing surfaces
- Progressive material removal
- Increases clearance and vibration

**Visual Appearance:**
- Scratches and grooves in rolling direction
- Dull, matte surface finish
- May show scoring lines
- Uniform wear pattern across raceway width
- Reduced raceway depth

**Sources of Contamination:**
- **External:** Dirt, sand, dust entering through seals
- **Internal:** Wear particles from other components
- **Lubricant:** Contaminated grease or oil
- **Manufacturing:** Residual grinding particles

**Severity Levels:**
- **Mild:** Fine scratches, minimal dimensional change
- **Moderate:** Visible grooves, measurable clearance increase
- **Severe:** Deep scoring, significant material loss, bearing unusable

**Measurement:**
- Increased radial clearance
- Surface roughness increase
- Dimensional checks of raceway depth

**Prevention:**
- Effective sealing
- Lubricant filtration (β ratio > 200 for particles > 6μm)
- Clean mounting environment
- Regular lubricant analysis and change
- Proper seal maintenance

**SKF Damage Code:** Abrasive wear

### 2.2 Adhesive Wear (Адгезионный износ)

**Description:**
Material transfer between contacting surfaces due to insufficient lubrication film.

**Mechanism:**
- Metal-to-metal contact occurs
- Surface asperities weld together
- Material tears off and transfers to opposing surface
- Creates rough surface and accelerates wear

**Visual Appearance:**
- Smeared or wiped appearance
- Material transfer visible as patches
- Rough, torn surface texture
- May show discoloration from heat
- Asymmetric wear pattern

**Operating Conditions:**
- Boundary lubrication regime
- Insufficient lubricant supply
- Excessive load
- High temperatures reducing viscosity
- Starting/stopping cycles

**Related Phenomena:**
- **Scuffing:** Severe adhesive wear with material transfer
- **Galling:** Extreme form with significant surface damage
- **Seizing:** Complete adhesion preventing rotation

**Prevention:**
- Ensure adequate lubrication film
- Use EP (Extreme Pressure) additives
- Control operating temperature
- Avoid shock loads
- Proper running-in procedures

### 2.3 Corrosive Wear (Коррозионный износ)

**Description:**
Combined effect of chemical attack and mechanical wear removing protective oxide films.

**Mechanism:**
1. Corrosion forms oxide layer on surface
2. Rolling action removes oxide layer
3. Fresh metal exposed to continued corrosion
4. Cycle repeats, progressively removing material

**Visual Appearance:**
- Reddish-brown (rust) or dark discoloration
- Rough, etched surface texture
- Uniform material loss
- May show pitting combined with general wear

**Causes:**
- Moisture in lubricant or environment
- Acidic lubricant degradation products
- Galvanic corrosion
- Fretting combined with corrosion
- Aggressive chemicals

**Prevention:**
- Exclude moisture (effective sealing)
- Use corrosion-inhibited lubricants
- Regular lubricant change
- Avoid dissimilar metals
- Control operating temperature
- Proper storage conditions (VCI bags for long-term)

**ГОСТ 20407-75:** Коррозионный износ

## 3. Plastic Deformation

### 3.1 True Brinelling (Истинное бринеллирование)

**Description:**
Permanent indentations in raceways caused by static overload exceeding material yield strength.

**Mechanism:**
- Static or shock load exceeds C0 rating
- Plastic deformation of raceway material
- Rolling elements create indentations at their contact points
- Permanent damage remains after load removal

**Visual Appearance:**
- Regular indentations matching rolling element spacing
- Indentations visible on both raceways and rolling elements
- Sharp, well-defined edges
- Spacing matches cage pocket distance
- May show circumferential or axial displacement

**Typical Scenarios:**
- Drop impact during handling
- Heavy shock load during operation
- Improper pressing force during mounting
- Hammering on bearing during installation
- Clamping force during transport

**Measurement:**
- Indentation depth typically 10-100μm
- Can be measured with profilometer
- Spacing matches rolling element pitch

**Consequences:**
- Vibration at frequency: n × (RPM/60) × Z
  - n = shaft speed (RPM)
  - Z = number of rolling elements
- Noise during operation
- Accelerated fatigue at indentation sites
- Reduced bearing life

**Prevention:**
- Avoid static loads > C0
- Proper handling and transport (supports at both rings)
- Lock shaft and housing during transport
- Use proper mounting tools (hydraulic fitters, induction heaters)
- Never hammer directly on bearing
- Install spring washers if vibration during transport is unavoidable

**ISO 15243:** Indentations from overload

### 3.2 False Brinelling (Ложное бринеллирование)

**Description:**
Wear damage resembling brinelling but caused by vibration-induced fretting when bearing is stationary.

**Mechanism:**
- Micro-movements between rolling elements and raceways
- Vibration or oscillation while bearing is not rotating
- Fretting wear at contact points
- Oxidized wear debris (red/brown) trapped in indentations

**Visual Appearance:**
- Indentations at rolling element spacing
- Red-brown coloration (iron oxide debris)
- Duller edges than true brinelling
- Elliptical shape in load direction
- May extend across full raceway if vibration is multi-directional

**Typical Scenarios:**
- Transport vibration (rail, truck, ship)
- Nearby equipment vibration
- Loosely fitted bearing experiencing vibration
- Long-term storage with vibration
- Electric motor coasting at critical speed

**Distinction from True Brinelling:**
| Feature | True Brinelling | False Brinelling |
|---------|----------------|------------------|
| Cause | Static overload | Vibration/fretting |
| Depth | Deeper, plastic deformation | Shallower, wear |
| Color | Shiny, metallic | Red-brown (oxide) |
| Edges | Sharp | Dull |
| Debris | None | Red oxide powder |

**Prevention:**
- Rotate bearing periodically during storage
- Eliminate vibration sources
- Ensure proper fits (interference)
- Apply preload if possible
- Use vibration isolation during transport
- Fill bearing with lubricant for transport
- Consider spring-loaded shaft for transport

**Lubrication Effect:**
- Proper lubrication reduces false brinelling
- Grease acts as cushion and fretting protection
- Oil-lubricated bearings more susceptible

### 3.3 Indentation from Debris (Вмятины от частиц)

**Description:**
Permanent deformations caused by hard particles pressed into raceways by rolling elements.

**Mechanism:**
- Hard particle (metal chip, dirt) enters bearing
- Rolling element presses particle into raceway
- Creates indentation matching particle shape
- May cause raised edges around indentation

**Visual Appearance:**
- Random, irregular indentations
- Not at regular intervals like brinelling
- May show particle embedded in indentation
- Raised material at edges
- Shiny bottom if recent, may corrode if old

**Particle Sources:**
- Assembly debris
- Seal damage allowing contamination
- Wear particles from other components
- Damaged cage fragments
- Contaminated lubricant

**Consequences:**
- Stress concentration at indentation
- Fatigue crack initiation sites
- Increased vibration
- Accelerated spalling
- Noise generation

**Prevention:**
- Cleanliness during assembly
- Effective sealing
- Lubricant filtration
- Inspect lubricant regularly
- Proper maintenance procedures

### 3.4 Overrolling Damage

**Description:**
Surface deformation from rolling over obstacles such as debris or damaged areas.

**Visual Appearance:**
- Localized deformation
- May show ripple pattern
- Material displacement
- Associated with foreign object

**Prevention:**
- Remove damaged bearings immediately
- Prevent debris entry
- Inspect associated components

## 4. Cracking Failures

### 4.1 Fatigue Cracking (Усталостные трещины)

**Description:**
Cracks developing from cyclic stress, typically progressing to spalling.

**Types:**
- Circumferential cracks (normal to rolling direction)
- Axial cracks (parallel to rolling direction)
- Network cracking

**Typical Locations:**
- Raceway surface
- Raceway shoulders
- Inner ring bore
- Cage pockets

**Detection:**
- Visual inspection (magnification)
- Dye penetrant testing
- Magnetic particle inspection
- Eddy current testing

**Prevention:**
- Proper bearing selection
- Avoid overloading
- Adequate lubrication
- Control mounting stresses

### 4.2 Thermal Cracking (Термические трещины)

**Description:**
Cracks caused by excessive heat or thermal cycling creating thermal stress.

**Mechanism:**
- Rapid temperature changes
- Thermal expansion mismatch
- Material phase changes from overheating
- Thermal stress exceeds material strength

**Visual Appearance:**
- Network of fine cracks (checking pattern)
- Circumferential cracks in rings
- Blue/brown discoloration indicating overheating
- Cracks perpendicular to heat flow direction

**Common Causes:**
- Inadequate lubrication causing friction heat
- External heat source (welding nearby)
- Electric current passage (arcing)
- Seized bearing overheating
- Rapid cooling after overheating

**Associated Damage:**
- Discoloration (blue, brown, black)
- Softened material (reduced hardness)
- Dimensional changes from expansion
- Microstructure changes

**Prevention:**
- Adequate lubrication and cooling
- Avoid electric current through bearing
- Protect from external heat sources
- Monitor temperature
- Proper mounting avoiding binding

### 4.3 Overload Cracking (Трещины от перегрузки)

**Description:**
Immediate fracture from single excessive load event.

**Characteristics:**
- Large, propagating cracks
- May result in ring fracture
- Typically originates at stress concentration
- Brittle fracture appearance

**Typical Scenarios:**
- Shock load far exceeding rating
- Improper mounting creating stress
- Press-fit too tight causing hoop stress
- Impact during handling

**Fracture Origins:**
- Inner ring: Bore (mounting groove if present)
- Outer ring: Outside diameter features
- Stress concentrations: Shoulders, grooves, holes

**Prevention:**
- Avoid shock loads
- Proper mounting procedures
- Correct fits and tolerances
- Gradual heating for mounting (not excessive)
- Inspect mounting features for stress concentrations

### 4.4 Grinding Cracks (Шлифовочные трещины)

**Description:**
Manufacturing defect - fine cracks from improper grinding causing local overheating.

**Visual Appearance:**
- Very fine network of cracks
- Perpendicular to grinding direction
- May require etching to make visible
- Located on ground surfaces (raceways, faces)

**Causes:**
- Excessive grinding heat
- Improper grinding parameters
- Insufficient coolant
- Dull grinding wheel
- Manufacturing quality issue

**Concern:**
- Indicates material transformation (untempered martensite)
- Reduced fatigue strength
- Potential early failure
- Quality control failure

**Detection:**
- Acid etching reveals cracks
- Magnetic particle inspection
- Typically found during incoming inspection

**Action:**
- Reject bearing if found
- Contact manufacturer
- Quality complaint

## 5. Corrosion Failures

### 5.1 Moisture Corrosion (Коррозия от влаги)

**Description:**
Rusting of bearing surfaces due to water or moisture exposure.

**Visual Appearance:**
- Red-brown rust on surfaces
- Pitting corrosion
- Uniform or localized attack
- May extend into material

**Severity Levels:**
- **Light:** Surface staining, easily cleaned
- **Moderate:** Pitting, surface roughness
- **Severe:** Deep pitting, material loss, bearing unusable

**Sources of Moisture:**
- Condensation from temperature cycling
- Water in lubricant
- Poor sealing allowing humid air
- Washing/cleaning procedures
- Steam or water leaks
- Extended storage in humid environment

**Prevention:**
- Effective sealing (contact seals)
- Corrosion-inhibited lubricants
- Controlled storage environment
- Desiccant breathers for housings
- Proper preservation for storage
- Regular operation (prevents condensation)

**ГОСТ 20407-75:** Общая коррозия

### 5.2 Fretting Corrosion (Фреттинг-коррозия)

**Description:**
Corrosion damage at fitting surfaces from micro-motion between ring and shaft/housing.

**Mechanism:**
- Micro-slip at mating surfaces
- Oxide formation from friction and oxygen
- Red/black oxide debris generation
- Progressive material removal
- Can occur at ring faces or fitting surfaces

**Visual Appearance:**
- Red-brown or black powder at interface
- Polished wear pattern
- Circumferential marks
- Visible when bearing is removed
- Discoloration of shaft or housing bore

**Common Locations:**
- Inner ring bore to shaft
- Outer ring OD to housing bore
- Ring end faces to shoulders

**Causes:**
- Loose fit (insufficient interference)
- Vibration during operation
- Cyclic loading causing deflection
- Improper fit tolerances
- Thin-walled housings deflecting

**Consequences:**
- Difficult bearing removal
- Shaft/housing damage
- Loosening of fit
- Increased vibration
- Eventual bearing rotation on shaft (creep)

**Prevention:**
- Proper interference fits per manufacturer specs
- Adequate shaft/housing rigidity
- Circlips or locknuts preventing axial movement
- Proper surface finish on seats
- Anti-fretting compounds on fits
- Avoid resonance frequencies

**ISO 15243:** Fretting on fitting surfaces

### 5.3 Electrical Erosion (Электроэрозия)

**Description:**
Damage from electric current passing through bearing, creating arc discharge.

**Mechanism:**
1. Voltage potential across bearing
2. Breakdown of lubricant film
3. Arc discharge between ring and rolling element
4. Localized melting/vaporization
5. Crater formation

**Visual Appearance:**
- **Fluting:** Closely spaced corrugations (washboard pattern)
  - Longitudinal grooves on raceway
  - Regular spacing (1-5mm typically)
  - Shiny, melted appearance
  - Covers large areas
  
- **Pitting:** Random small craters
  - Scattered pits on raceways
  - Welded spots
  - Frosted appearance
  - May progress to fluting

**Common Sources:**
- VFD (Variable Frequency Drive) motors without proper grounding
- Welding current path through bearing
- Static electricity buildup
- Magnetic field asymmetry
- Improper grounding

**Progression:**
1. Initial random pitting
2. Pits coalesce into grooves
3. Fluting pattern develops
4. Rapid deterioration
5. Noisy operation, early failure

**Detection:**
- Distinctive visual pattern
- Characteristic noise (buzzing, crackling)
- Elevated vibration
- Microscopic examination shows melted craters

**Prevention:**
- **Electrical insulation:** Insulated bearings (ceramic coating)
- **Grounding:** Shaft grounding brushes/rings
- **VFD measures:** 
  - Shielded cables
  - Common-mode chokes
  - dV/dt filters
  - Proper grounding scheme
- **Avoid current path through bearing**
- **Conductive grease** (can worsen problem - not recommended)

**Standards:**
- IEC 60034-17 (Cage induction motors - bearing currents)
- IEEE 112 (Test procedures for motors)

### 5.4 Etching (Травление)

**Description:**
Chemical attack on bearing surfaces from acidic lubricant or environment.

**Causes:**
- Lubricant degradation (oxidation producing acids)
- Acidic contamination
- Chemical exposure
- Additives incompatible with materials

**Visual Appearance:**
- Dull, etched surface
- Loss of surface finish
- May show patterns
- Discoloration

**Prevention:**
- Use fresh, appropriate lubricant
- Regular lubricant analysis (TAN - Total Acid Number)
- Avoid contamination
- Compatible materials and lubricants

## 6. Smearing and Scoring

### 6.1 Smearing (Смазывание)

**Description:**
Material transfer and surface damage from sliding contact under load.

**Mechanism:**
- Inadequate lubrication film
- Sliding occurs instead of pure rolling
- Surface welding and tearing
- Material transfer between surfaces

**Common Scenarios:**
- **Starting torque:** High load at low speed (thin film)
- **Acceleration:** Rapid speed change causing slip
- **Vibration:** Oscillation causing sliding
- **Skidding:** Rolling elements sliding on raceway

**Visual Appearance:**
- Streaks in sliding direction
- Discoloration (blue, brown from heat)
- Rough, torn surface
- Material transfer patches
- Typically on both raceways and rolling elements

**Locations:**
- Race edges (rib contacts)
- Rolling element ends (spherical roller bearings)
- Cage pockets (sliding contact)
- High slip regions

**Prevention:**
- Adequate lubrication, especially at startup
- Proper preload preventing slip
- Controlled acceleration
- EP lubricant additives
- Running-in procedures
- Avoid excessive vibration

### 6.2 Scoring (Задиры)

**Description:**
Severe surface damage from gross sliding under heavy load with inadequate lubrication.

**Characteristics:**
- Deep scratches and grooves
- Significant material displacement
- May show welding traces
- Heat discoloration
- Often catastrophic

**Causes:**
- Seizure (bearing locked)
- Severe overload with film breakdown
- Contamination with large particles
- Lubricant failure

**Prevention:**
- Ensure adequate lubrication
- Avoid severe overloading
- Cleanliness
- Proper operating conditions

## 7. Cage Failures

### 7.1 Cage Wear (Износ сепаратора)

**Description:**
Material removal from cage pockets, pillars, or surfaces.

**Mechanisms:**
- Friction with rolling elements
- Sliding against rings or shoulders
- Abrasive contamination
- Inadequate lubrication of cage

**Visual Appearance:**
- Polished pockets
- Reduced material thickness
- Elongated pockets
- Wear debris

**Consequences:**
- Increased clearance between cage and elements
- Cage instability
- Noise and vibration
- Potential cage breakage

**Prevention:**
- Adequate lubrication reaching cage
- Proper cage material selection
- Cleanliness
- Avoid excessive speeds

### 7.2 Cage Fracture (Поломка сепаратора)

**Description:**
Complete break of cage structure.

**Common Causes:**
- Fatigue from cyclic loading
- Impact from rolling element slip
- Overheating causing material degradation
- Excessive forces from misalignment
- Corrosion weakening
- Manufacturing defects

**Typical Fracture Locations:**
- Pillar/bar sections
- Pocket edges
- Rivets (riveted cages)
- Welds (welded cages)

**Consequences:**
- Immediate bearing failure
- Rolling elements may contact each other
- Potential catastrophic damage
- Debris generation

**Visual Inspection:**
- Fracture surface examination
- Check for fatigue beach marks
- Look for corrosion
- Check for overheating signs
- Inspect remaining cage for cracks

**Prevention:**
- Proper bearing selection (cage type and material)
- Avoid excessive speed, acceleration
- Adequate lubrication
- Control operating temperature
- Proper mounting (avoid misalignment)

### 7.3 Cage Overheating (Перегрев сепаратора)

**Description:**
Thermal damage to cage, particularly polymer cages.

**Signs:**
- Discoloration (brown, black)
- Softening, deformation
- Brittle fracture
- Burned smell

**Causes:**
- Excessive speed
- Inadequate lubrication
- Friction from misalignment
- External heat source

**Material Sensitivity:**
- Polymer cages: Most sensitive (typical limit 120°C)
- Brass cages: Moderate (up to 250°C)
- Steel cages: High resistance (300°C+)

**Prevention:**
- Monitor temperature
- Ensure adequate lubrication
- Select appropriate cage material
- Control speed
- Proper alignment

## 8. Seal Failures

### 8.1 Seal Wear (Износ уплотнения)

**Description:**
Degradation of seal lip or sealing surface.

**Mechanisms:**
- Friction wear of lip
- Aging/hardening of elastomer
- Chemical attack
- Temperature degradation

**Consequences:**
- Loss of sealing effectiveness
- Lubricant leakage
- Contamination entry
- Subsequent bearing failure

**Signs:**
- Visible lip wear
- Lubricant leakage
- Hardening, cracking
- Reduced contact pressure

### 8.2 Seal Damage (Повреждение уплотнения)

**Description:**
Physical damage compromising seal integrity.

**Common Damage:**
- Torn lip from improper installation
- Cut from sharp edges
- Deformed from excessive press fit
- Displaced from position

**Prevention:**
- Proper installation techniques
- Chamfer housing bore entry
- Avoid seal contact with sharp edges
- Use installation tools
- Check alignment

## Visual Identification Guide

### Surface Appearance Summary

| Failure Mode | Surface Appearance | Color | Location Pattern |
|--------------|-------------------|-------|------------------|
| Normal fatigue | Shallow spalls, shiny facets | Metallic, shiny | Load zone, circumferential |
| Pitting | Small craters, frosted | Dull grey | Distributed on raceway |
| Abrasive wear | Scratches, grooves | Dull, matte | Uniform across width |
| Adhesive wear | Smeared, wiped | Discolored patches | Asymmetric |
| True brinelling | Regular indentations | Shiny | Rolling element spacing |
| False brinelling | Elliptical wear | Red-brown (oxide) | Rolling element spacing |
| Moisture corrosion | Pitting, uniform rust | Red-brown rust | General or localized |
| Fretting corrosion | Polished, powder | Red-black powder | Fit surfaces, faces |
| Electrical erosion | Fluting/pitting | Shiny, frosted | Raceways, widespread |
| Smearing | Streaks, material transfer | Heat colors | Sliding contact areas |
| Thermal cracks | Fine crack network | Blue/brown discolor | General |

## Failure Mode Decision Tree

### Step 1: Type of Damage

**Is there material removal?**
- Yes → Go to Step 2
- No → Go to Step 3

### Step 2: Material Removal Pattern

**Spalling/chunks missing:**
- In load zone only → Normal fatigue (expected at end of life)
- Random location → Subsurface fatigue, check for inclusions
- Deep, irregular → Overload or poor material quality

**Fine surface wear:**
- Scratches/grooves → Abrasive wear (contamination)
- Smooth, uniform removal → Corrosive wear
- Pits and craters → Pitting, micropitting, or electrical erosion

**Smeared appearance:**
- Sliding marks → Smearing, adhesive wear
- Material transfer → Inadequate lubrication

### Step 3: Deformation or Cracking

**Indentations present:**
- Regular spacing, shiny → True brinelling (overload)
- Regular spacing, red oxide → False brinelling (vibration)
- Random, irregular → Debris indentation

**Cracks visible:**
- Fine network → Thermal cracking (overheating)
- Large cracks → Overload fracture
- Very fine, ground surface → Grinding cracks (manufacturing)

**Corrosion:**
- Red-brown rust → Moisture corrosion
- Red-black powder at fits → Fretting corrosion
- Fluting pattern → Electrical erosion
- Etched surface → Chemical attack

### Step 4: Color and Discoloration

| Color | Indication |
|-------|------------|
| Metallic, shiny | Normal fatigue, brinelling |
| Red-brown rust | Moisture corrosion |
| Red-brown oxide powder | False brinelling, fretting |
| Blue/brown/black | Overheating (> 200-300°C) |
| Dull grey, frosted | Pitting, micropitting, wear |
| Shiny fluting | Electrical erosion |
