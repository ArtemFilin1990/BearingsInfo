# Bearing Mounting and Dismounting

## Purpose
This document provides comprehensive guidance on proper bearing mounting and dismounting techniques, tools, procedures, and safety precautions for different bearing types and applications.

## Mounting Preparation

### Pre-Mounting Inspection

**Bearing Inspection:**

1. **Packaging Integrity:**
   - Check for damage to packaging
   - Verify preservation intact
   - Confirm no signs of corrosion or contamination

2. **Visual Inspection:**
   - Examine raceways for damage, rust, or discoloration
   - Check rolling elements for dents or scratches
   - Inspect cage for damage or deformation
   - Verify seals/shields properly installed (if applicable)

3. **Rotation Check:**
   - Rotate bearing by hand
   - Should rotate smoothly and quietly
   - No roughness, binding, or noise
   - Light preload or resistance is normal for sealed bearings

4. **Identification Verification:**
   - Confirm bearing designation matches requirement
   - Check accuracy class markings
   - Verify clearance code (if specified)

**Shaft and Housing Inspection:**

1. **Dimensional Check:**
   - Measure shaft diameter at multiple points
   - Measure housing bore diameter
   - Verify within specified tolerance
   - Check roundness (out-of-round < 50% of tolerance)

2. **Surface Condition:**
   - Check for burrs, nicks, or scratches
   - Verify surface finish (Ra ≤ 1.6 μm for shafts, Ra ≤ 3.2 μm for housings)
   - Remove any damage with fine emery cloth
   - Clean thoroughly after repair

3. **Geometry:**
   - Check shoulder perpendicularity (< 0.001 × d)
   - Verify fillet radii (must be < bearing corner radius)
   - Check shaft and housing alignment

4. **Cleanliness:**
   - Must be free of dirt, chips, old lubricant
   - No rust or corrosion
   - Paint, coatings removed from bearing seats

### Cleaning Procedures

**Bearing Cleaning:**

**For Preserved Bearings:**
- Remove preservation grease with:
  - White spirit (mineral spirit)
  - Kerosene
  - Commercial bearing cleaner
- **Do not use:** Chlorinated solvents, strong acids/alkalis

**Cleaning Process:**
1. Immerse bearing in clean solvent
2. Rotate bearing slowly by hand
3. Do not spin bearing rapidly (can damage cage)
4. For heavily preserved bearings, use warm solvent (40-50°C)
5. Blow dry with clean, dry compressed air
6. Apply light oil immediately to prevent rust

**For Pre-Lubricated/Sealed Bearings:**
- **Do not wash** - lubricated at factory
- Wipe exterior only if needed
- Mount directly

**Shaft and Housing Cleaning:**
1. Degrease with solvent
2. Remove all dirt, chips, old lubricant
3. Dry thoroughly
4. Apply thin film of oil to prevent rust
5. Keep clean until mounting

### Tools and Equipment Preparation

**Essential Tools:**

- Clean, lint-free cloths
- Appropriate solvents and oils
- Calibrated measuring instruments (micrometers, bore gages)
- Mounting tools (discussed below)
- Clean work area
- Protective gloves (lint-free)
- Safety glasses

**Workspace:**
- Clean, dust-free area
- Adequate lighting
- Stable work surface
- All tools within reach

## Cold Mounting Methods

### Hand Pressing (Light Interference)

**Suitable For:**
- Very light interference fits (j5, j6, light k6)
- Small bearings (bore < 30 mm)
- Minimal force required

**Procedure:**
1. Clean and oil shaft lightly
2. Align bearing squarely on shaft
3. Push bearing onto shaft by hand
4. Ensure bearing seats against shoulder

**Advantages:**
- Simple, no special tools
- No risk of overheating

**Limitations:**
- Very limited applications
- Only lightest fits

### Drift and Hammer Method

**Equipment:**
- Soft-faced hammer (brass, plastic, or rawhide)
- Tubular drift or mounting sleeve
- Must contact ring being pressed (not both rings)

**Critical Rule:**
**Always apply force to ring with interference fit, never through rolling elements**

**For Shaft Mounting (Inner Ring Interference):**

**Procedure:**
1. Clean shaft and bearing
2. Apply light oil to shaft
3. Place bearing against shoulder
4. Position drift against **inner ring face only**
   - Drift must not contact outer ring or cage
   - Diameter: slightly less than inner ring land
5. Strike drift evenly around circumference
6. Move drift progressively (like clock positions)
7. Continue until bearing seats against shoulder

**For Housing Mounting (Outer Ring Interference):**

**Procedure:**
1. Apply force to **outer ring only**
2. Use large diameter drift or tube
3. Must not contact inner ring
4. Strike evenly around circumference

**Common Errors:**
- **Hammering on wrong ring** - damages raceways
- **Hammering through rolling elements** - causes brinelling
- **Uneven striking** - cocks bearing, damages cage
- **Excessive force** - cracks rings

**Advantages:**
- Simple, low cost
- Suitable for field service
- No power required

**Disadvantages:**
- Risk of damage if improper technique
- Difficult for large bearings
- Tiring for operator
- Not recommended for precision bearings

### Press Mounting

**Equipment:**
- Arbor press or hydraulic press
- Mounting sleeves/rings (correct diameter)
- Press bed and supports

**Critical Requirements:**
- **Press must be aligned** - no cocking
- **Apply force to interference ring only**
- **Support opposite side properly**
- **Control force and speed**

**For Inner Ring Press:**

**Procedure:**
1. Position shaft vertically in press
2. Clean and oil bearing seat
3. Place bearing on shaft, align carefully
4. Position mounting sleeve against inner ring face
   - Sleeve OD < bearing OD
   - Sleeve ID > shaft diameter
   - Must contact inner ring only
5. Apply force smoothly and steadily
6. Monitor force (should not exceed bearing static load rating)
7. Press until bearing seats against shoulder

**Force Estimation:**
```
F ≈ π × d × b × μ × p
```
Where:
- F = mounting force (N)
- d = shaft diameter (mm)
- b = ring width (mm)
- μ = friction coefficient (0.1-0.2)
- p = contact pressure (MPa), related to interference

**Typical mounting forces:**
- Light interference (k6): 5-20 kN
- Medium interference (m6): 20-50 kN
- Heavy interference (n6): 50-150 kN

**For Outer Ring Press:**

**Procedure:**
1. Place housing in press
2. Position bearing in housing bore
3. Use mounting sleeve against outer ring
   - Must contact outer ring only
   - ID > bearing bore diameter
4. Press until seated

**Advantages:**
- Controlled force
- Minimal risk of damage (if done correctly)
- Suitable for medium/large bearings
- Repeatable process

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires press equipment
- Mounting sleeves needed
- Not portable

### Hydraulic Mounting Tools

**Oil Injection Method:**

**Principle:**
- High-pressure oil injected between shaft and bearing bore
- Creates temporary clearance
- Bearing slides on easily

**Equipment:**
- Hydraulic pump (up to 100-150 MPa)
- Shaft with oil distribution grooves and radial holes
- Pressure gauge

**Procedure:**
1. Position bearing on prepared shaft (with oil holes/grooves)
2. Connect hydraulic pump to oil passage
3. Apply pressure gradually
4. Bearing bore expands slightly
5. Push bearing onto shaft (minimal force needed)
6. Release pressure - bearing grips shaft
7. Ensure bearing seated against shoulder

**Advantages:**
- Very clean mounting
- Minimal force required
- Excellent for large bearings (d > 200 mm)
- No heat required
- Reduces risk of damage

**Disadvantages:**
- Shaft must be specially prepared
- Requires hydraulic equipment
- Initial cost of shaft modification

**Applications:**
- Large bearings
- Production environments
- Precision bearings
- Frequent mounting/dismounting

### Hydraulic Nuts

**Description:**
- Nut with built-in hydraulic cylinder
- Screws onto threaded shaft end
- Applies axial force to mount bearing

**Procedure:**
1. Thread hydraulic nut onto shaft
2. Position bearing
3. Pressurize nut - extends and contacts bearing
4. Bearing pushed onto shaft
5. Release pressure, remove nut

**Advantages:**
- Clean, controlled mounting
- No hammering
- Suitable for large bearings

**Disadvantages:**
- Shaft must be threaded
- Requires hydraulic pump
- Higher cost

## Hot Mounting Methods

### When to Use Hot Mounting

**Indications:**
- Medium to heavy interference fits (m6, n6, p6)
- Large bearings (bore > 80 mm)
- Minimize mounting force
- Avoid risk of press damage
- Frequent mounting/dismounting

**Principle:**
- Bearing (inner ring) heated
- Thermal expansion increases bore diameter
- Bearing slides onto shaft with clearance
- Cools and grips shaft

### Induction Heating (Recommended)

**Equipment:**
- Induction bearing heater
- Temperature monitoring (thermocouple or infrared)

**Procedure:**

1. **Setup:**
   - Place bearing on heater ring
   - Ensure even support (not on outer ring edge)
   - Set temperature limit (100-120°C max)

2. **Heating:**
   - Activate heater
   - Monitor temperature continuously
   - Heat uniformly and evenly
   - **Do not exceed 120°C** for standard bearings
   - Time: 5-20 minutes depending on bearing size

3. **Temperature Check:**
   - Verify temperature with thermocouple or temp stick
   - **Never exceed 120-130°C**
   - Overheating damages heat treatment

4. **Mounting:**
   - Using heat-resistant gloves, lift bearing
   - Quickly position on shaft
   - Push against shoulder (may need light force)
   - Hold in position until cooled (2-5 minutes)
   - Verify proper seating

5. **Cooling:**
   - Allow to cool naturally (air cooling)
   - **Do not quench** or accelerate cooling
   - Wait until bearing reaches ambient temperature

**Temperature Guidelines:**

| Bearing Bore (mm) | Heating Temp (°C) | Heating Time (min) |
|-------------------|-------------------|-------------------|
| 20-50 | 80-100 | 5-10 |
| 50-100 | 90-110 | 10-15 |
| 100-200 | 100-120 | 15-25 |
| > 200 | 110-120 | 25-40 |

**Advantages:**
- Clean, efficient heating
- Uniform temperature distribution
- Automatic demagnetization (some models)
- Temperature control
- Safe - no flame
- Fast

**Disadvantages:**
- Equipment cost
- Requires electrical power
- Not portable (some models)

**Best Practice for Modern Applications**

### Oil Bath Heating

**Equipment:**
- Oil bath container (clean)
- Heating element with thermostat
- Support grid (bearing must not touch bottom)
- Thermometer
- Clean mineral oil (no additives)

**Procedure:**

1. **Preparation:**
   - Fill container with clean oil
   - Set up support grid 50-100 mm from bottom
   - Install thermometer
   - Heat oil to 80-100°C

2. **Heating Bearing:**
   - Suspend bearing on support grid
   - Bearing must not touch bottom or sides
   - Monitor temperature
   - Heat for 15-30 minutes
   - Do not exceed 100-120°C

3. **Mounting:**
   - Remove bearing from oil (drain excess)
   - Wipe clean with cloth
   - Mount quickly on shaft
   - Seat against shoulder

4. **Cleanup:**
   - Wipe excess oil from bearing and shaft
   - Allow to cool

**Advantages:**
- Uniform heating
- Simple equipment
- Good temperature control

**Disadvantages:**
- Messy (oil handling)
- Fire risk if overheated
- Requires cleanup
- Slower than induction
- Environmental concerns

**Safety Notes:**
- Use only appropriate oil (flash point > 200°C)
- Never use water (can cause explosions with hot oil)
- Have fire extinguisher nearby

### Hot Plate Method

**Equipment:**
- Electric hot plate with thermostat
- Heat-resistant gloves

**Procedure:**
1. Set hot plate to 100-120°C
2. Place bearing flat on hot plate
3. Monitor temperature
4. Heat 10-20 minutes (depending on size)
5. Mount on shaft

**Advantages:**
- Simple, low cost
- Portable

**Disadvantages:**
- Uneven heating (one side hotter)
- Difficult to control temperature
- Can damage seals/shields
- Not recommended for precision bearings

**Use only for emergency field repairs**

### Heating Guidelines and Limits

**Maximum Temperature:**
- **Standard bearings: 120°C maximum**
- **Sealed bearings: 80-100°C** (protect grease and seals)
- **Precision bearings: 100-110°C**
- **Carburized bearings: 150°C maximum** (can use higher temp)

**Overheating Consequences:**
- Tempering of races (loss of hardness)
- Dimensional instability
- Reduced bearing life
- Grease degradation (sealed bearings)
- Seal damage

**Temperature Check Methods:**
- Thermocouple (most accurate)
- Infrared thermometer
- Temperature indicating crayons/sticks
- Built-in heater display

**Required Expansion:**

For interference δ, to get mounting clearance of ~0.1-0.2 mm:

```
ΔT = (δ + clearance) / (α × d)
```

Example: 100 mm bore, 50 μm interference
```
ΔT = (0.05 + 0.1) / (12×10⁻⁶ × 100) = 125°C
```

Temperature rise of 80-100°C usually sufficient for most fits

## Mounting Procedures by Bearing Type

### Deep Groove Ball Bearings

**Typical Mounting:**

**Single Bearing (Locating Side):**
1. Mount inner ring with appropriate fit (k6, m6)
2. Use hot mounting if m6 or tighter
3. Seat firmly against shaft shoulder
4. Mount outer ring in housing (H7 clearance)
5. Clamp axially (locknut, end cap)

**Opposed Bearing (Non-Locating):**
1. Allow axial displacement in housing (G7) or shaft (h6)
2. Provides thermal expansion compensation

**With Seals/Shields:**
- Do not heat above 80°C (protects grease and seals)
- Consider cold press mounting
- Be careful not to damage seals during mounting

### Angular Contact Ball Bearings

**Single Row:**

**Mounting Considerations:**
- Direction specific (load direction)
- Must be axially preloaded in operation
- Usually mounted in sets (pairs)

**Procedure:**
1. Clean and inspect
2. Mount inner ring on shaft (typically k5 or k6)
3. Mount outer ring in housing (H6 or J6)
4. Apply preload via:
   - Spacer rings (between bearing sets)
   - Lock nuts with precise axial positioning
   - Spring preload

**Duplex Pairs (Back-to-Back, Face-to-Face):**

**Pre-Matched Sets:**
- Factory matched and ground
- Mount together as a unit
- Do not separate or interchange
- Correct spacer rings provided

**Mounting:**
1. Keep matched set together
2. Observe directional markings
3. Apply specified preload
4. Verify smooth rotation

**Universal Matching:**
- Individual bearings ground to standard
- Can be paired in field
- Use precision spacers

### Cylindrical Roller Bearings

**Separable Design:**
- Inner ring with rollers and cage separates from outer ring
- Simplifies mounting

**Mounting Procedure:**

**Method 1 - Inner Ring Assembly onto Shaft:**
1. Heat or press inner ring assembly onto shaft
2. Seat against shoulder
3. Install shaft into housing with outer ring
4. Outer ring loose in housing (H7)

**Method 2 - Outer Ring into Housing:**
1. Press outer ring into housing (if interference fit)
2. Install inner ring assembly on shaft
3. Insert shaft assembly into housing

**For NU, N-types (no shoulders):**
- Requires external axial retention
- Use retaining rings, end caps, or locknuts

**For NJ, NUP types (flanges):**
- Integral flange provides one-side axial location
- Opposite side requires retention

### Tapered Roller Bearings

**Separable Design:**
- Inner ring (cone) with rollers
- Outer ring (cup) separate

**Mounting and Adjustment:**

**Single Bearing:**
1. Press cup into housing (usually M7)
2. Heat and mount cone on shaft (usually k6 or m6)
3. Assemble into housing
4. Adjust axial clearance/preload

**Opposed Pairs:**

**Direct Mounting (face-to-face):**
1. Mount both cups in housing
2. Mount both cones on shaft
3. Adjust clearance with:
   - Shim pack
   - Adjustable nut
   - Spacer length

**Indirect Mounting (back-to-back):**
1. Mount cups and cones
2. Use outer ring spacers for adjustment
3. Tighten lock nut to specification

**Setting Clearance:**

**Procedure:**
1. Install bearings with shims or spacers
2. Tighten to specification
3. Rotate shaft several revolutions
4. Measure:
   - Axial play (feeler gauge)
   - Rotational torque
5. Adjust shims/spacers to achieve:
   - Specified clearance (typically 0.02-0.05 mm)
   - Or specified preload (for precision applications)

**Running-In:**
- After adjustment, run bearing under light load
- Recheck clearance after 30-60 minutes
- Readjust if necessary

### Spherical Roller Bearings

**Mounting:**

**Self-Aligning Feature:**
- Outer ring raceway is spherical
- Allows compensation for shaft misalignment (±1-2°)
- Inner ring must be firmly seated

**Procedure:**

1. **Inner Ring:**
   - Usually tight fit (m6 or n6 for large bearings)
   - Hot mounting recommended for d > 100 mm
   - Seat firmly against shaft shoulder

2. **Outer Ring:**
   - Usually clearance fit (H7)
   - Must be free to self-align
   - No clamping of outer ring (allow movement)

3. **Lubrication:**
   - Fill with grease before mounting (if not pre-lubricated)
   - Large bearings: oil lubrication recommended

**Large Bearings (d > 300 mm):**
- Use oil injection method
- Or induction heating
- Hydraulic nuts for dismounting
- May require special lifting equipment

### Thrust Bearings

**Thrust Ball Bearings:**

**Single Direction:**
1. One washer with groove (rotating)
2. One flat washer (stationary)
3. Balls and cage between

**Mounting:**
1. Identify rotating and stationary washers
2. Mount rotating washer with appropriate fit
3. Stationary washer with clearance fit
4. Ensure perpendicularity (critical)
5. Preload as specified

**Double Direction:**
- Two ball sets
- Three washers
- Center washer typically rotating

**Thrust Roller Bearings:**

**Cylindrical Thrust:**
- Must be perfectly perpendicular to shaft axis
- Highly sensitive to misalignment
- Use precision shoulders and faces

**Tapered Thrust (Large bearings):**
- Often split for assembly
- Careful alignment required
- Heavy press fits may be needed

## Dismounting Methods

### General Principles

**Critical Rules:**
1. **Never force through rolling elements**
2. **Pull from interference ring only**
3. **Use appropriate tools** (not hammers and chisels)
4. **Avoid damage to shaft and housing**
5. **Plan before starting**

### Mechanical Pullers

**Two-Jaw and Three-Jaw Pullers:**

**For Pulling Inner Ring from Shaft:**

**Requirements:**
- Puller jaws must grip inner ring only
- Must not contact outer ring, cage, or rolling elements
- Puller screw bears on shaft end (with protective cap)

**Procedure:**
1. Remove any retaining devices (nuts, circlips)
2. Position puller jaws under inner ring
3. Ensure even grip around circumference
4. Protect shaft end with hardened disc/cap
5. Tighten puller screw gradually
6. Apply steady force, not impact
7. May use light tapping on puller screw
8. Bearing will release suddenly - be prepared

**For Pulling Outer Ring from Housing:**
- Use puller with expanding jaws
- Or housing with extraction grooves
- Pull outer ring from behind

**Special Puller Features:**
- **Extraction grooves** on bearing or housing
- **Internal puller** for blind housings
- **Hydraulic pullers** for large bearings

**Advantages:**
- Controlled removal
- No heat required
- Minimal risk to shaft/housing

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires access behind bearing
- May damage bearing (acceptable if replacing)
- Limited by space constraints

### Hydraulic Methods

**Hydraulic Nuts:**

**For Shaft-Mounted Bearings:**

**Procedure:**
1. Remove locknut/retaining device
2. Thread hydraulic nut onto shaft
3. Nut contacts inner ring
4. Pressurize nut - pushes bearing off shaft
5. Control pressure and movement
6. Remove bearing

**Advantages:**
- Clean, controlled
- No impact
- Preserves shaft
- Excellent for large bearings

**Disadvantages:**
- Shaft must be threaded
- Requires hydraulic pump
- Equipment cost

**Oil Injection Method:**

**For Shafts with Oil Distribution System:**

**Procedure:**
1. Connect hydraulic pump to oil passage
2. Increase pressure to 50-100 MPa
3. Bearing bore expands slightly
4. Pull bearing off with light force
5. Release pressure

**Advantages:**
- Minimal force required
- No bearing damage
- Very clean removal
- Excellent for large bearings

**Requirements:**
- Shaft with oil grooves and radial holes
- Hydraulic pump

### Induction Heating for Dismounting

**For Inner Rings:**

**Principle:**
- Heat inner ring
- Thermal expansion reduces interference
- Pull off with minimal force

**Procedure:**

**For Separable Bearings:**
1. Remove outer ring
2. Heat inner ring assembly with induction heater
3. Heat to 100-120°C
4. Pull or tap off shaft

**For Non-Separable Bearings:**
1. Use split induction coil (if available)
2. Heat inner ring in situ
3. Pull with mechanical puller simultaneously
4. Requires specialized equipment

**Advantages:**
- Reduces force required
- Preserves shaft finish
- Good for stubborn bearings

**Disadvantages:**
- Requires induction equipment
- Not suitable for all bearing types
- Can damage bearing (if reusing, be careful)

### Pressing Off

**Using Press:**

**Procedure:**
1. Support bearing/housing properly
2. Position press ram against inner ring (for shaft mounting)
3. Apply force steadily
4. Use appropriate support so force goes through interference ring only

**Caution:**
- Easy to damage bearing or shaft
- Use only if puller not feasible
- Support carefully to avoid bending shaft

## Common Mounting Errors and Prevention

### Error 1: Hammering Through Rolling Elements

**Consequence:**
- Brinelling of raceways (permanent indentations)
- Immediate or premature failure

**Prevention:**
- Always use mounting sleeve or drift
- Contact correct ring only (interference fit ring)
- Never strike cage or rolling elements

### Error 2: Cocking During Mounting

**Consequence:**
- Bearing not fully seated
- Uneven load distribution
- Cage damage
- Premature failure

**Prevention:**
- Ensure bearing square to shaft
- Apply force evenly around circumference
- Use proper mounting tools
- Check seating against shoulder

### Error 3: Overheating

**Consequence:**
- Loss of hardness (tempering)
- Dimensional changes
- Reduced life
- Seal/grease damage

**Prevention:**
- Monitor temperature continuously
- Never exceed 120°C (standard bearings)
- Use temperature-controlled equipment
- Verify temperature before mounting

### Error 4: Contamination During Mounting

**Consequence:**
- Dirt in bearing
- Accelerated wear
- Premature failure

**Prevention:**
- Clean work area
- Clean parts thoroughly before mounting
- Keep bearing wrapped until ready to mount
- Use clean gloves
- Apply clean lubricant

### Error 5: Incorrect Fit Selection

**Consequence:**
- Ring creep (if too loose)
- Excessive stress (if too tight)
- Reduced clearance, preload issues

**Prevention:**
- Verify fit selection per application
- Measure shaft and housing dimensions
- Confirm within tolerance
- Use fit tables and guidelines

### Error 6: Damage to Seals/Shields

**Consequence:**
- Loss of lubrication
- Contamination entry
- Short bearing life

**Prevention:**
- Handle carefully during mounting
- Do not overheat sealed bearings (max 80°C)
- Protect seals from impact
- Install seal facing correctly

### Error 7: Inadequate Lubrication

**Consequence:**
- Immediate damage
- Overheating
- Failure

**Prevention:**
- Lubricate before or immediately after mounting
- Use correct lubricant type and quantity
- For grease: fill to correct percentage
- For oil: ensure oil reaches bearing before operation

## Safety Precautions

### Personal Safety

**Personal Protective Equipment (PPE):**
- Safety glasses (always)
- Heat-resistant gloves (for hot mounting)
- Steel-toed boots
- Hearing protection (for press operations)
- Protective clothing

**Handling:**
- Use proper lifting techniques
- Get help for heavy bearings (>25 kg)
- Use lifting equipment for very large bearings
- Secure workpieces properly

### Equipment Safety

**Press Safety:**
- Keep hands clear of press area
- Use guards and shields
- Inspect tools before use
- Do not exceed press capacity

**Heating Safety:**
- Do not overheat oil (fire risk)
- Fire extinguisher nearby
- Ventilation for fumes
- Electrical safety for induction heaters

**Tool Safety:**
- Inspect pullers for cracks or damage
- Use correct size tools
- Secure puller properly before applying force
- Be prepared for sudden release

### Environmental Safety

**Waste Disposal:**
- Dispose of used oil properly
- Recycle old bearings
- Clean up spills immediately

**Ventilation:**
- Adequate ventilation when using solvents
- Fume extraction for heating operations

## Step-by-Step Procedures

### Procedure 1: Mounting Small Bearing with k6 Fit (Cold Press)

**Bearing: 6206 (d=30 mm), Fit: 30k6 shaft, 62H7 housing**

**Steps:**

1. **Preparation (10 minutes):**
   - Clean shaft and housing thoroughly
   - Measure shaft: verify 30.013-30.029 mm
   - Measure housing bore: verify 62.000-62.030 mm
   - Inspect bearing: rotate by hand, check for smooth operation
   - Apply light oil film to shaft

2. **Mounting Inner Ring:**
   - Position bearing on shaft, aligned squarely
   - Place mounting sleeve (OD 50-60 mm, ID 32-35 mm) against inner ring face
   - Position assembly in arbor press
   - Apply force smoothly and steadily
   - Press until bearing seats against shoulder
   - Force required: approximately 5-10 kN

3. **Mounting Assembly into Housing:**
   - Align shaft assembly with housing
   - Insert carefully (H7 clearance fit - slides in)
   - Ensure bearing outer ring seats properly

4. **Final Steps:**
   - Install retaining devices (snap ring, end cap)
   - Lubricate bearing (3-4 grams grease for 6206)
   - Rotate by hand - should turn smoothly
   - Install seals, covers

**Time: ~15-20 minutes**

---

### Procedure 2: Mounting Large Bearing with m6 Fit (Induction Heating)

**Bearing: 6316 (d=80 mm), Fit: 80m6 shaft, 170H7 housing**

**Steps:**

1. **Preparation (15 minutes):**
   - Measure shaft: verify within 80.033-80.054 mm range
   - Measure housing: verify 170.000-170.040 mm
   - Clean all surfaces
   - Inspect bearing
   - Prepare induction heater

2. **Heating Bearing:**
   - Place bearing flat on heater ring
   - Set temperature limit: 110°C
   - Activate heater
   - Monitor temperature with IR thermometer
   - Heat for 15-20 minutes until 100-110°C reached

3. **Mounting:**
   - Using heat-resistant gloves, lift bearing
   - Align carefully with shaft
   - Slide onto shaft quickly
   - Push firmly against shoulder
   - Hold in position for 2-3 minutes
   - Bearing will grip shaft as it cools

4. **Cooling and Final Assembly:**
   - Allow to cool naturally (15-20 minutes)
   - Do not accelerate cooling
   - Once cool, install shaft into housing
   - Install retaining devices
   - Lubricate (approximately 15-18 grams grease)
   - Final rotation check

**Time: ~45-60 minutes**

---

### Procedure 3: Dismounting Bearing with Two-Jaw Puller

**Removing inner ring from shaft**

**Steps:**

1. **Preparation:**
   - Remove retaining nut, lock washer, or snap ring
   - Clean area around bearing
   - Select appropriate puller size

2. **Position Puller:**
   - Place puller jaws behind inner ring
   - Ensure jaws grip inner ring only (not outer ring or cage)
   - Position evenly (2 or 3 jaw contact points)
   - Center puller screw on shaft end
   - Place hardened cap on shaft end (protect threads)

3. **Apply Force:**
   - Tighten puller screw gradually
   - Apply steady force, not jerky movements
   - May tap puller screw lightly with hammer while under tension
   - Continue tightening

4. **Removal:**
   - Bearing will suddenly release
   - Be prepared to support bearing as it comes off
   - Remove puller and bearing

5. **Inspection:**
   - Inspect shaft for damage
   - Check for burrs or raised metal
   - Clean shaft
   - Apply protective oil if not immediately installing new bearing

**Time: ~10-15 minutes**
