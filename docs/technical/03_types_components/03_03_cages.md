# Bearing Cages (Separators/Retainers)

## Purpose
This document describes bearing cage functions, types, materials, and selection criteria.

## Cage Functions

### Primary Functions

1. **Separate Rolling Elements**
   - Prevent rolling element contact
   - Eliminate element-to-element friction and wear
   - Reduce noise and vibration

2. **Guide Rolling Elements**
   - Guide elements through loaded zone
   - Guide elements through unloaded zone
   - Maintain proper element spacing (pitch)

3. **Distribute Lubricant**
   - Transport lubricant into bearing
   - Distribute grease or oil to contact areas
   - Improve lubrication efficiency

4. **Maintain Element Position**
   - Hold elements during handling and assembly
   - Prevent element scatter during operation
   - Facilitate bearing mounting

## Cage Types by Construction

### 1. Stamped (Pressed) Steel Cages

**Description:**
Manufactured from steel sheet by stamping and forming operations.

**Construction:**
- Two half-cages pressed from steel sheet
- Riveted or welded together
- Pockets formed by pressing
- Low weight

**Materials:**
- Low-carbon steel (St3, 08кп)
- Plated for corrosion protection (zinc, cadmium, or nickel)

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Economical - lowest cost
- ✔ Lightweight
- ✔ Good for high speeds
- ✔ Mass production suitable

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Lower strength than machined cages
- ✘ Limited pocket guidance accuracy
- ✘ Not suitable for very high loads
- ✘ Limited operating temperature range

**Applications:**
- Deep groove ball bearings (60xx, 62xx series)
- Small to medium ball bearings
- Electric motors
- General machinery
- High-speed applications

**Speed Capability:** Very high (up to 80-90% of bearing speed limit)

### 2. Machined Metal Cages

**Description:**
Precision-machined from solid metal (brass, bronze, steel).

**Construction:**
- Machined from solid bar stock or tube
- CNC machining for precision
- Accurate pockets
- One-piece or two-piece design

**Materials:**

**Brass (ЛС59-1, C38500):**
- Good machinability
- Excellent heat dissipation
- Natural lubricity
- Moderate strength
- Operating temp: -40°C to +200°C

**Bronze (БрА9Ж3Л, БрОФ):**
- Higher strength than brass
- Excellent wear resistance
- Good heat resistance
- Higher cost
- Operating temp: -40°C to +250°C

**Steel (St3, 20):**
- High strength
- Lower cost than brass
- Requires surface treatment
- Operating temp: -40°C to +150°C

**Advantages:**
- ✔ High strength and rigidity
- ✔ Precise pocket guidance
- ✔ Suitable for heavy loads
- ✔ Good heat dissipation (brass, bronze)
- ✔ Long service life

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Higher cost than stamped cages
- ✘ Heavier weight
- ✘ More manufacturing time

**Applications:**
- Large bearings
- Heavy-duty applications
- Cylindrical roller bearings
- Tapered roller bearings
- Angular contact bearings (precision)
- High-temperature environments

**Speed Capability:** Moderate to high

### 3. Molded Polymer Cages

**Description:**
Injection-molded from engineering polymers.

**Materials:**

**Polyamide 66 (PA66, Nylon 66):**
- Most common polymer cage material
- Glass-fiber reinforced (typical: 30% GF)
- Good strength-to-weight ratio
- Self-lubricating properties
- Operating temp: -40°C to +120°C

**PEEK (Polyetheretherketone):**
- High-performance polymer
- Excellent chemical resistance
- High temperature capability
- Operating temp: -60°C to +250°C
- Higher cost

**PPS (Polyphenylene sulfide):**
- Good chemical resistance
- High temperature capability
- Operating temp: -40°C to +180°C

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Very lightweight (lowest density)
- ✔ Excellent quiet running (noise reduction)
- ✔ Self-lubricating properties
- ✔ Corrosion resistant
- ✔ No metal-to-metal contact
- ✔ Complex geometries possible
- ✔ Cost-effective for mass production

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Lower strength than metal cages
- ✘ Limited operating temperature
- ✘ Moisture absorption (PA66)
- ✘ Not suitable for very heavy loads

**Applications:**
- Electric motors (quiet operation)
- Automotive applications
- Household appliances
- Low-noise requirements
- Corrosive environments
- Mass production bearings

**Speed Capability:** Moderate to high

### 4. Ribbon (Window-Type) Cages

**Description:**
Simple cage design for cylindrical roller bearings, consisting of metal strips connecting roller pockets.

**Construction:**
- Axial bars connected by cross-members
- Large windows for rollers
- Minimal material
- Steel or brass

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Simple, economical
- ✔ High filling capacity (more rollers)
- ✔ Good lubrication circulation
- ✔ Lightweight

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Lower guidance accuracy
- ✘ Less rigid

**Applications:**
- Cylindrical roller bearings
- Simple designs
- Cost-sensitive applications

### 5. Snap Cages

**Description:**
Two-piece plastic or metal cages that snap together during assembly.

**Construction:**
- Two halves with interlocking features
- Snap-fit connection
- No rivets or welding required

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Easy assembly
- ✔ No additional fasteners
- ✔ Cost-effective

**Applications:**
- Thin-section bearings
- Easy assembly requirements

### 6. Crown (Pin-Type) Cages

**Description:**
Cage for cylindrical or tapered rollers with axial pins that fit between rollers.

**Construction:**
- Pins extend axially
- Fit in annular grooves between rollers
- Guided by ring or rollers

**Applications:**
- Tapered roller bearings
- Some cylindrical roller bearings

## Cage Guidance

### 1. Ring-Guided Cages

**Guidance Surface:**
Cage guided by inner or outer ring land (non-raceway surface).

**Characteristics:**
- More stable operation
- Better for high speeds
- Less sensitive to roller tolerance variations
- Requires precision ring surfaces

**Typical Use:**
- Ball bearings
- High-speed applications
- Precision bearings

### 2. Roller-Guided (Ball-Guided) Cages

**Guidance Surface:**
Cage pockets guided by rolling elements themselves.

**Characteristics:**
- Simpler design
- Less demanding ring manufacturing
- More sensitive to element size variations
- Higher cage wear potential

**Typical Use:**
- Roller bearings
- Moderate speed applications
- Cost-sensitive designs

## Cage Materials Selection

### Temperature Considerations

| Material | Max Operating Temp | Applications |
|----------|-------------------|--------------|
| PA66 (Nylon) | +120°C | Standard, low noise |
| Steel (plated) | +150°C | General purpose |
| Brass | +200°C | High temp, good cooling |
| Bronze | +250°C | High temp, heavy duty |
| PEEK | +250°C | High temp, chemical resistant |
| Phenolic | +280°C | Extreme high temp |

### Load Considerations

**Light to Moderate Loads:**
- Polymer cages suitable
- Stamped steel cages

**Heavy Loads:**
- Machined brass or bronze cages
- Machined steel cages

**Shock Loads:**
- Metal cages preferred
- Avoid brittle materials

### Speed Considerations

**High Speed (n·dm > 500,000):**
- Lightweight polymer cages
- Stamped steel cages
- Ring-guided preferred

**Moderate Speed:**
- All cage types suitable

**Low Speed:**
- Any cage type
- Load capacity more important

### Environment Considerations

**Corrosive Environments:**
- Polymer cages (PA66, PEEK)
- Stainless steel cages
- Bronze cages

**High Temperature:**
- Bronze cages
- PEEK cages
- Phenolic cages

**Low Noise Required:**
- Polymer cages (PA66)
- Stamped steel with low clearance

**Chemical Exposure:**
- PEEK cages
- PPS cages
- Bronze cages

## Cage Clearance and Fit

### Cage Clearance

**Definition:**
The gap between cage pocket and rolling element, or between cage and guiding surface.

**Purpose:**
- Allow for thermal expansion
- Accommodate manufacturing tolerances
- Permit element motion

**Too Small:**
- Risk of cage binding
- Increased friction
- Noise generation

**Too Large:**
- Cage instability
- Element skewing
- Noise and vibration

**Typical Values:**
- Pocket clearance: 0.1 to 0.5 mm (depending on bearing size)
- Guide clearance: 0.05 to 0.3 mm

## Cage Surface Treatments

### For Steel Cages

**Zinc Plating:**
- Corrosion protection
- Standard protection
- Low cost

**Cadmium Plating:**
- Better corrosion protection than zinc
- Good lubrication properties
- Environmental restrictions

**Nickel Plating:**
- Corrosion protection
- Wear resistance
- Aesthetic appearance

**Black Oxide:**
- Corrosion protection
- Break-in improvement
- Low cost

**Silver Plating:**
- Extreme conditions
- High temperature
- Low friction

### For Polymer Cages

**Glass Fiber Reinforcement:**
- Increased strength
- Dimensional stability
- Typical: 15-30% glass fibers

**Solid Lubricants:**
- PTFE (Teflon) addition
- Molybdenum disulfide
- Graphite
- Reduced friction

## Cage Failure Modes

### Common Failure Causes

1. **Wear:**
   - Inadequate lubrication
   - Contamination
   - Excessive clearances

2. **Fracture:**
   - Overload
   - Material defect
   - Fatigue

3. **Pocket Wear:**
   - Roller-guided cages
   - Inadequate lubrication
   - Contamination

4. **Softening (Polymer):**
   - Excessive temperature
   - Incompatible lubricant
   - Chemical attack

5. **Corrosion (Metal):**
   - Moisture ingress
   - Corrosive environment
   - Inadequate plating

### Prevention

- Proper lubrication selection and quantity
- Appropriate cage material for application
- Correct internal clearance
- Proper sealing
- Avoid overload conditions
- Regular maintenance

## Special Cage Designs

### Full Complement (No Cage)

**Description:**
Maximum number of rolling elements with no cage.

**Advantages:**
- Highest load capacity
- Maximum element count

**Disadvantages:**
- Element-to-element contact and friction
- Higher noise and wear
- Lower speed capability
- Not suitable for high-speed

**Applications:**
- Very heavy loads, low speeds
- Oscillating motion
- Limited space

### Segmented Cages

**Description:**
Cage divided into segments for large bearings.

**Advantages:**
- Easier assembly for large bearings
- Reduced manufacturing cost

**Applications:**
- Large industrial bearings
- Slewing bearings
