# Bearing Seals and Shields

## Purpose
This document describes bearing sealing solutions, their characteristics, and selection criteria for protecting bearings from contamination and retaining lubrication.

## Functions of Seals and Shields

### Primary Functions

1. **Prevent Contamination Entry**
   - Keep out dirt, dust, and particles
   - Block moisture and water
   - Exclude chemicals and solvents

2. **Retain Lubrication**
   - Keep grease inside bearing
   - Prevent oil leakage
   - Maintain lubricant quantity

3. **Protect from Environment**
   - Shield from splashing liquids
   - Guard against corrosive atmospheres
   - Block airborne contaminants

## Shields (Non-Contact Seals)

### Metal Shields (Z, ZZ)

**Description:**
Stamped metal plates attached to one bearing ring with a small clearance gap to the other ring.

**Construction:**
- **Z** - shield on one side only
- **ZZ (or 2Z)** - shields on both sides
- Typically stamped from sheet steel
- Pressed into groove in outer ring
- Small clearance gap (0.2-0.5 mm) to inner ring

**Characteristics:**
- **Non-contact design** - no friction
- **No speed limitation** from shield itself
- **No wear** - infinite life
- **Basic protection** against large particles
- **Does not retain grease well** - some grease can escape

**Gap:**
- Typical radial clearance: 0.2 to 0.5 mm
- Allows air circulation
- Prevents large particle entry

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Zero friction (no contact)
- ✔ Suitable for very high speeds
- ✔ No heat generation from sealing
- ✔ Long life (no wear)
- ✔ Low cost

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Limited sealing effectiveness
- ✘ Cannot retain grease effectively
- ✘ Not suitable for dusty environments
- ✘ Cannot prevent liquid ingress
- ✘ Requires external sealing in harsh conditions

**Applications:**
- Clean environments
- High-speed applications
- Applications where external sealing is provided
- Initial grease charge only (not sealed for life)
- Low-contamination risk

**Speed Capability:** Unlimited (no contact)

**Designation:**
- **-Z** - one shield
- **-ZZ** or **-2Z** - two shields

## Contact Seals (Rubbing Seals)

### Standard Rubber Seals (RS, 2RS)

**Description:**
Elastomeric (rubber) seals that make light contact with the bearing ring.

**Construction:**
- Molded rubber or synthetic elastomer
- Metal reinforcement ring (steel stamping)
- Pressed into groove in outer ring
- Sealing lip contacts inner ring

**Sealing Types:**

**RS (or RSL, RSR) - One Seal:**
- Single-sided sealing
- One side open or shielded

**2RS (or 2RSL, 2RSR) - Two Seals:**
- Both sides sealed
- Sealed for life
- Permanent grease charge

**Materials:**

**Nitrile Rubber (NBR, Buna-N):**
- Most common seal material
- Good oil and grease resistance
- Operating temp: -40°C to +100°C (short-term +120°C)
- Economical

**Fluoroelastomer (FKM, Viton):**
- Excellent heat resistance
- Chemical resistant
- Operating temp: -20°C to +200°C
- Higher cost

**Hydrogenated Nitrile (HNBR):**
- Better heat resistance than NBR
- Good chemical resistance
- Operating temp: -40°C to +150°C

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Excellent sealing effectiveness
- ✔ Prevents water and fine particle ingress
- ✔ Retains grease very well
- ✔ Sealed-for-life capability
- ✔ No external sealing usually needed
- ✔ Simple, compact

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Contact friction (slight torque increase)
- ✘ Speed limitation (typically n·dm < 500,000)
- ✘ Seal wear over time
- ✘ Temperature limited by rubber material
- ✘ Cannot be regreasable (in sealed bearings)

**Applications:**
- Dusty environments
- Water exposure
- General machinery
- Electric motors
- Sealed-for-life applications
- Maintenance-free requirements

**Speed Capability:** 
- Standard seals: n·dm up to 300,000-500,000
- Low-friction seals: n·dm up to 500,000-700,000

**Contact Pressure:**
- Light spring or rubber pressure
- Typical: 0.01 to 0.05 MPa
- Torque increase: 5% to 20% of bearing friction

**Designation:**
- **-RS** or **-RSL** - one low-friction seal
- **-2RS** or **-2RSL** - two low-friction seals
- **-RSR** or **-2RSR** - one/two reinforced seals (higher contact pressure)

### Low-Friction Seals (RSL, LLU, RZ)

**Description:**
Contact seals optimized for lower friction and higher speeds.

**Design Features:**
- Reduced contact pressure
- Optimized lip geometry
- Low-friction rubber compounds
- Sometimes multiple lips

**Advantages:**
- Lower friction than standard seals
- Higher speed capability
- Good sealing still maintained

**Applications:**
- Higher speed requirements (than standard RS)
- Energy-efficient applications
- Electric vehicles

**Speed Capability:** n·dm up to 500,000-700,000

### Labyrinth Seals (Non-Contact or Near-Contact)

**Description:**
Complex path seals that block contamination without direct contact.

**Construction:**
- Multiple turns or gaps
- Tortuous path for contaminants
- May incorporate grease packing
- Metal or rubber-metal combination

**Types:**

**Gap-Type Labyrinth:**
- Very small gap (0.05-0.2 mm)
- No contact
- Multiple stages

**Grease-Packed Labyrinth:**
- Labyrinth filled with grease
- Grease acts as barrier
- Renewable in some designs

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Very low friction
- ✔ High speed capability
- ✔ Good sealing with proper design
- ✔ Long life

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ More complex design
- ✘ Higher cost
- ✘ Requires precise manufacturing

**Applications:**
- High-speed applications
- High-temperature applications
- Low-friction requirements

**Speed Capability:** Very high (n·dm > 700,000)

## Integrated Seals (Bearing with Seals)

### Sealed-for-Life Bearings (2RS, 2RZ)

**Description:**
Bearings with integral contact seals and lifetime grease fill.

**Characteristics:**
- Factory-filled with grease
- Seals prevent grease escape and contamination entry
- No relubrication possible
- Maintenance-free

**Grease Fill:**
- Typically 25% to 35% of free volume
- Lithium-based or polyurea grease
- Selected for long life

**Life:**
- Grease life typically limiting factor
- 10,000 to 40,000 hours typical (depends on speed and temperature)

**Advantages:**
- ✔ No maintenance required
- ✔ Clean operation
- ✔ Simplified design
- ✔ Lower total cost of ownership

**Disadvantages:**
- ✘ Limited life by grease
- ✘ Cannot be replenished
- ✘ Speed limited by seals

**Applications:**
- Electric motors (maintenance-free)
- Appliances
- Automotive components
- Difficult-to-access locations

### Shielded Bearings (ZZ, 2Z)

**Description:**
Bearings with integral metal shields and grease fill.

**Characteristics:**
- Factory-filled with grease
- Shields provide basic protection
- May allow regreasing through relief slots
- Higher speed than sealed bearings

**Applications:**
- Clean to moderately dirty environments
- High-speed requirements
- Initial grease charge sufficient

## External Sealing Solutions

### V-Ring Seals

**Description:**
Axially-acting elastomeric seal pressed onto shaft, sealing against a counterface.

**Installation:**
- Slips onto shaft
- No groove required
- Sealing lip contacts housing or cover

**Advantages:**
- ✔ Easy installation
- ✔ No machining required
- ✔ Good for retrofit
- ✔ Effective against dirt and water

**Applications:**
- Retrofit sealing
- Open bearings
- Harsh environments
- Agricultural equipment

### Lip Seals (Radial Shaft Seals)

**Description:**
Independent seals mounted in housing bore, sealing against shaft.

**Types:**
- Single lip
- Double lip
- Spring-loaded
- PTFE lip (high speed/temperature)

**Applications:**
- High-performance sealing
- Separate from bearing
- Chemical environments
- High speeds (PTFE)

### Felt Seals

**Description:**
Wool felt rings providing basic sealing for slow speeds.

**Characteristics:**
- Low cost
- Low friction
- Grease-impregnated
- Slow speeds only

**Applications:**
- Low-speed applications
- Cost-sensitive
- Basic protection

## Seal Selection Criteria

### By Operating Conditions

#### Clean Environment
- **Recommendation:** Shields (ZZ) or no seals
- **Reasoning:** Minimal contamination risk, maximizes speed

#### Moderately Dirty
- **Recommendation:** One seal + one shield (RSZ)
- **Reasoning:** Balance of protection and speed

#### Very Dusty / Dirty
- **Recommendation:** Two contact seals (2RS) + external sealing
- **Reasoning:** Maximum protection needed

#### Water Exposure
- **Recommendation:** Two contact seals (2RS), FKM material
- **Reasoning:** Prevent water ingress, good water resistance

#### High Temperature
- **Recommendation:** FKM seals or labyrinth seals
- **Reasoning:** NBR temperature limit ~100°C

#### High Speed
- **Recommendation:** Shields (ZZ) or low-friction seals (RSL) or labyrinth
- **Reasoning:** Minimize friction

#### Maintenance-Free Requirement
- **Recommendation:** Sealed-for-life (2RS) bearings
- **Reasoning:** Grease retained, no service needed

### By Speed Range (n·dm)

| Speed Range | Recommended Sealing |
|------------|---------------------|
| < 100,000 | Any seal type |
| 100,000 - 300,000 | Standard seals (2RS), shields (ZZ) |
| 300,000 - 500,000 | Low-friction seals (RSL), shields |
| 500,000 - 700,000 | Low-friction seals, labyrinth, shields |
| > 700,000 | Shields only, or labyrinth |

### By Temperature Range

| Seal Material | Temperature Range | Application |
|--------------|-------------------|-------------|
| NBR (Nitrile) | -40°C to +100°C | Standard applications |
| HNBR | -40°C to +150°C | Higher temperature |
| FKM (Viton) | -20°C to +200°C | High temperature, chemicals |
| PTFE | -200°C to +250°C | Extreme temperature |
| Metal shields | -40°C to +150°C+ | No polymer limit |

## Seal Performance

### Friction Torque

**Shields (ZZ):**
- Negligible friction
- No measurable torque increase

**Contact Seals (2RS):**
- Torque increase: 10% to 30% of bearing basic friction
- Torque decreases slightly during break-in
- Low-friction seals: 5% to 15% increase

### Grease Retention

**Shields:**
- Poor long-term retention
- Grease can migrate out
- Regular regreasing may be needed

**Contact Seals:**
- Excellent retention
- Sealed-for-life capable
- Minimal grease loss

### Contamination Exclusion

**Effectiveness Rating:**

| Seal Type | Dust Exclusion | Water Exclusion | Fine Particle Exclusion |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------|------------------------|
| No seal | Poor | Poor | Poor |
| Shields (ZZ) | Fair | Poor | Poor |
| One seal (RS) | Good | Good | Good |
| Two seals (2RS) | Excellent | Excellent | Excellent |
| Labyrinth | Good-Excellent | Fair-Good | Excellent |

## Seal Installation and Handling

### Installation Tips

1. **Avoid Damage:**
   - Do not strike seals directly
   - Use proper installation tools
   - Protect seal lip during assembly

2. **Cleanliness:**
   - Keep shaft and housing clean
   - Remove burrs and sharp edges
   - Inspect for damage before installation

3. **Alignment:**
   - Ensure proper alignment
   - Avoid cocking or tilting
   - Seat fully in groove

### Seal Inspection

**Before Installation:**
- Check for cracks or damage
- Verify proper size and type
- Ensure seal lip flexibility

**After Installation:**
- Verify full seating
- Check for gaps or deformation
- Ensure no interference with rotation

## Common Seal Problems and Solutions

### Problem: Seal Leaking

**Causes:**
- Damaged seal lip
- Improper installation
- Shaft surface rough or damaged
- Excessive shaft runout

**Solutions:**
- Replace seal
- Ensure proper installation procedure
- Improve shaft surface finish
- Check alignment

### Problem: High Torque

**Causes:**
- Excessive seal contact pressure
- Seal interference too high
- Wrong seal type

**Solutions:**
- Use low-friction seal variant
- Verify correct seal specification
- Consider shields if sealing less critical

### Problem: Premature Seal Wear

**Causes:**
- High speed
- High temperature
- Contamination
- Poor lubrication

**Solutions:**
- Use appropriate seal material
- Reduce speed or use shields
- Improve external sealing
- Ensure adequate lubrication
